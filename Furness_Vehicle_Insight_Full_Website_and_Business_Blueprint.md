# 1. Executive brief

## 1.1 The business we are building

Furness Vehicle Insight must not look, sound or operate like an ordinary mobile mechanic. It should be positioned as a **mobile automotive intelligence and inspection business** that helps vehicle owners, used-car buyers, dealers, garages and small fleets make better decisions using documented technical evidence.

The business visits the vehicle where practical, performs a clearly scoped assessment, records what was tested and observed, explains the significance of findings, and issues a professional digital report. Where appropriate, it can also provide onward repair recommendations or refer work to a suitably qualified partner. The business does not need to make repair labour its main source of revenue.

The commercial opportunity is to sell expertise, independent evidence, risk reduction, fleet visibility and recurring oversight—not just an hour of someone plugging in a scan tool.

## 1.2 Strategic positioning

**Brand promise:** Clear answers. Better vehicle decisions.

**Primary proposition:** Independent mobile vehicle diagnostics, inspections and condition intelligence for customers and businesses across Furness.

**Supporting proposition:** We turn vehicle symptoms, inspection observations and diagnostic data into clear, documented findings that customers can act on.

**Positioning statement:** Furness Vehicle Insight is an independent mobile vehicle intelligence service serving Dalton-in-Furness, Barrow-in-Furness, Ulverston and the wider Furness area. We provide evidence-led diagnostics, detailed vehicle inspections, EV battery assessments through suitable specialist methods, and ongoing fleet condition reporting.

## 1.3 Four principal revenue engines

1. **Premium consumer vehicle intelligence** — pre-purchase inspections, vehicle health assessments, diagnostic investigations, second opinions and EV battery condition reports where the vehicle and test method are supported.
2. **Recurring fleet condition intelligence** — monthly contracts for local businesses with cars and vans. Provide a fleet register, condition records, defect escalation, scheduled checks, service/MOT reminders, inspection history and management reporting. This supports the customer's maintenance system; it does not replace the operator's statutory responsibilities or any required safety inspection.
3. **Dealer and motor-trade services** — multi-vehicle stock days, pre-sale condition reports, diagnostic checks, EV battery testing through an approved partner or suitable equipment, and trade-facing report access.
4. **Advanced diagnostic engineering** — complex electrical and electronic fault investigation, structured test plans and evidence-led diagnostic support for independent garages and repairers.

## 1.4 The signature product: Vehicle Intelligence Passport

Every eligible inspected vehicle can receive a secure digital **Vehicle Intelligence Passport**: a time-stamped, vehicle-linked history of FVI inspections and reports.

It should contain:

- Vehicle identity details recorded during the assessment.
- Inspection date, mileage and location where appropriate.
- Inspection scope and exclusions.
- Technician identity and relevant competence declaration.
- Diagnostic equipment/method used where relevant.
- Fault codes and the context in which they were retrieved.
- Photographs and annotated evidence.
- Individual findings and severity levels.
- Recommendations and follow-up actions.
- Previous FVI reports, where the customer has authorised their inclusion.
- A unique report ID and QR/verification link.

The Passport is an FVI record of work performed and findings observed. It is **not** an MOT certificate, statutory roadworthiness certificate, manufacturer warranty, guarantee of future reliability or official government vehicle record. A QR code verifies that a report was issued by FVI and has not been altered; it must not reveal private customer data to an unauthorised viewer.

## 1.5 What success looks like

The website must generate qualified enquiries and bookings, while the platform behind it must make the business operationally repeatable. The longer-term objective is a balanced mix of consumer work, trade work and contracted fleet revenue so that income is not dependent only on one-off private bookings.

# 2. Business strategy and differentiation

## 2.1 Do not compete as “another mobile mechanic”

The website must avoid leading with generic phrases such as “all repairs undertaken”, “cheap diagnostics” or “mobile mechanic near you”. Those claims put the business in a crowded price-led category.

FVI should lead with:

- Independent vehicle assessment.
- Evidence-led diagnostics.
- Clear written reports.
- Vehicle purchase risk assessment.
- EV battery condition insight, using suitable test methods.
- Fleet condition visibility and reporting.
- Specialist diagnostic support for the motor trade.

The business may add selected repairs later, but the primary identity should remain inspection, diagnostics and intelligence.

## 2.2 Differentiators that must be built into the service

### A. Vehicle Intelligence Passport

A persistent, secure digital record for a vehicle that has been inspected by FVI. It creates a reason for customers to return for an annual or pre-sale update and gives dealers a documented evidence pack. Each new inspection adds a dated record rather than overwriting the previous one.

### B. Evidence-first reports

Reports must go beyond a list of fault codes. Each material finding should show:

- What was reported by the customer.
- What was inspected or tested.
- What was observed or measured.
- The limits of the test.
- Whether the finding is confirmed, suspected or not reproduced.
- The recommended next step.
- Supporting photographs, measurements or diagnostic snapshots where suitable.

Never describe a fault code as proof that the component named by the code has failed. The report must distinguish code retrieval, diagnosis and confirmed root cause.

### C. Fleet Uptime & Condition Desk

A recurring B2B offer for small and medium businesses operating cars and vans. The business receives a live register of its vehicles, upcoming dates, reported defects, FVI inspection results and a monthly exception report. The service is about making fleet condition visible and helping managers organise follow-up—not promising that breakdowns will never happen.

### D. Dealer Stock Intelligence Days

A scheduled mobile visit to a dealer or trade site to inspect several vehicles in one trip. Provide consistent reports, prioritised findings and optional EV battery health reports for compatible vehicles. Multi-car bookings improve technician utilisation and reduce travel time per vehicle.

### E. Trade Diagnostic Engineering Desk

A specialist service for garages that have already carried out initial checks but need help with complex electrical or electronic faults. FVI supplies a documented test plan, on-site investigation where appropriate, diagnostic evidence and a clear handover. This must operate within technician competence, equipment access, manufacturer security access and agreed trade terms.

### F. EV Battery Confidence Assessment

Offer battery state-of-health testing only after selecting and obtaining training/access to a suitable independent testing platform or approved partner arrangement. Verify vehicle compatibility before accepting payment. The report must identify the testing method, equipment/provider, test limitations and the fact that a battery health result is an assessment at a point in time—not a guarantee of future capacity or range.

### G. Independent recommendations, not repair upselling

FVI should be able to recommend further testing, a repairer or a specialist without creating pressure to sell the repair itself. Where FVI receives a referral fee or has another commercial relationship, disclose it. Independence is a core brand asset and must be protected.

## 2.3 Competitive positioning matrix

| Typical garage/mobile mechanic offer | FVI's intended offer |
|---|---|
| Basic scan and verbal explanation | Structured investigation, documented evidence and a clear report |
| Repair-led service | Independent information and decision support first |
| One-off vehicle check | Vehicle Passport with dated inspection history |
| General service list | Distinct consumer, dealer, fleet and trade products |
| Fleet maintenance arranged informally | Fleet condition dashboard, exception reporting and planned check schedule |
| EV battery estimate from a generic scan | Supported specialist battery test with transparent method and limitations |
| Garage technician relies on own workflow | Trade diagnostic case, test plan and evidence handover |

Do not claim FVI is the first, only, certified, approved or market-leading provider unless that statement has been verified and can be substantiated.

# 3. Target markets and customer profiles

## 3.1 Private vehicle owners

**Need:** Understand a warning light, electrical issue, unusual symptom or general vehicle condition without immediately authorising repairs.

**Products:** Mobile diagnostics, advanced fault investigation, vehicle health assessment, second opinion.

**Website message:** “Understand what is happening before deciding what to do next.”

## 3.2 Used-car buyers

**Need:** Reduce the risk of buying a vehicle with hidden or expensive problems.

**Products:** Pre-purchase inspection, comprehensive buyer report, optional vehicle-history check through a licensed third-party provider, optional EV battery assessment.

**Website message:** “Know more about the vehicle before you commit to buying it.”

The inspection is not a guarantee that a vehicle is fault-free. It is a scoped assessment of accessible items and available evidence at a particular time.

## 3.3 Used-car dealers and independent traders

**Need:** A repeatable way to inspect stock, identify issues before sale, document condition and provide additional buyer confidence.

**Products:** Dealer Stock Intelligence Day, individual stock report, EV battery report where supported, reinspection, trade portal.

**Website message:** “Consistent, independent vehicle evidence for your stock.”

## 3.4 Small business fleets

Target businesses with approximately 3–50 cars or light commercial vehicles initially: trades, service companies, local delivery operators, property maintenance businesses, care providers and other firms where vehicles are operationally important.

**Need:** Know which vehicles need attention, see inspection history, record defects, coordinate follow-up and reduce avoidable operational disruption.

**Products:** Fleet Uptime & Condition Desk, scheduled fleet visits, condition assessments, defect escalation and management reporting.

**Website message:** “Know the condition of your vehicles before they disrupt your working day.”

## 3.5 Independent garages and repairers

**Need:** Support with faults that are outside their current diagnostic capacity, equipment access or available technician time.

**Products:** Trade Diagnostic Engineering Desk, on-site diagnostic investigation, remote case review where evidence is sufficient, written technical handover.

**Website message:** “Specialist diagnostic support when the fault is not straightforward.”

## 3.6 Leasing, rental and vehicle remarketing businesses

Later-stage market. Potential work includes return-condition assessments, resale preparation reports, vehicle intake checks and multi-vehicle inspection days. Develop only after service capacity, report methodology and commercial insurance are proven.

# 4. Products, packages and service boundaries

All prices below are **launch hypotheses in GBP**, not verified local market rates. Test them against technician time, travel, equipment/software costs, insurance, VAT status, report administration and actual demand. Show “from” prices only where scope can genuinely vary; otherwise show a fixed price and clear inclusions.

## 4.1 Consumer product range

### Product 1 — Insight Scan

**Purpose:** An initial diagnostic assessment for a defined complaint.

May include:
- Customer symptom capture.
- Vehicle identification check.
- Full system scan to the extent supported by the vehicle and equipment.
- Relevant fault-code retrieval and status review.
- Selected live-data checks where appropriate.
- Initial findings and recommended next step.
- Short digital report.

Does not automatically include root-cause diagnosis, component removal, wiring repairs, coding, programming or a guarantee that every module is accessible.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £79–£99.

### Product 2 — Advanced Diagnostic Investigation

**Purpose:** Structured diagnosis of a defined problem, including electrical, intermittent or multi-system faults.

May include:
- Symptom reproduction attempt.
- Relevant wiring/system research where licensed information is available.
- Live-data and actuator testing where supported.
- Electrical measurements with suitable equipment.
- Test-plan documentation.
- Evidence and findings report.
- Further testing proposal if the fault is not conclusively resolved.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £145–£195 initial block, with additional time agreed before work continues.

### Product 3 — Vehicle Health Baseline

**Purpose:** A documented snapshot of vehicle condition for an existing owner.

May include:
- Visual exterior/interior checks.
- Tyres, visible brake condition and accessible under-bonnet checks.
- Battery and charging assessment where suitable.
- Warning-light review.
- Diagnostic scan where supported.
- Fluid/leak observations.
- Photographs and prioritised report.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £119–£159.

### Product 4 — Pre-Purchase Insight Inspection

**Purpose:** Independent condition assessment before a used vehicle purchase.

May include:
- Identity/VIN and registration consistency checks where accessible.
- Exterior/body/interior assessment.
- Visible corrosion, damage and repair-quality observations.
- Tyres and accessible brake/suspension observations.
- Engine bay and fluid/leak observations.
- Starting/operation checks where safe.
- Diagnostic scan within equipment capability.
- Road test only where legally permitted, insured, authorised and safe.
- Photo evidence.
- Prioritised findings and buyer summary.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £199–£249.

### Product 5 — Comprehensive Buyer Due-Diligence Pack

A premium offer combining the pre-purchase inspection with optional third-party vehicle history checks, additional diagnostic time, a detailed report and a buyer review call. Add an EV battery test only where compatible and delivered through an approved method/partner.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £279–£399 excluding any separately charged third-party data or EV test fee.

### Product 6 — Second Opinion Review

Review an existing garage quotation, report or diagnostic findings, followed by physical verification where needed.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £99–£149 desk/evidence review; physical investigation quoted separately.

### Product 7 — EV Battery Health Assessment

Launch only when FVI has a suitable provider/equipment route, competence, terms and insurance. Confirm exact vehicle support before taking payment.

Report may include the test-provider's SoH result, method, date, mileage, relevant cell/pack data where the method supports it, warranty information supplied or checked through an appropriate source, and limitations. Do not invent a universal pass threshold or describe the result as an official government certificate.

**Indicative retail hypothesis:** £99–£149 plus travel or provider costs, subject to partner pricing and vehicle coverage.

### Product 8 — Vehicle Passport Update

An annual or pre-sale refresh for a vehicle with an existing Passport. Recheck selected condition points and add a new dated assessment. It is not a substitute for servicing or statutory testing.

**Indicative price hypothesis:** £89–£139.

## 4.2 Trade and dealer products

### Dealer Stock Intelligence Day

A half-day or full-day booked site visit for multiple vehicles. Each vehicle receives a defined check and report. Batch pricing should reflect vehicle count, site access, vehicle condition, travel and the time required per unit.

Possible deliverables:
- Per-vehicle stock report.
- Diagnostic summary within supported vehicle coverage.
- Photo pack.
- Priority/attention schedule across the stock inspected.
- Optional EV battery tests via supported equipment/provider.
- Dealer portal with downloadable reports.

**Commercial model:** Site-day minimum plus per-vehicle fee. Do not promise unrealistic inspection throughput: pilot actual time per vehicle before quoting volume commitments.

### Trade Diagnostic Engineering Desk

- Initial case review with symptom, scan and test evidence.
- Fixed-scope remote evidence review where possible.
- On-site investigation by agreed time block.
- Written diagnostic findings and next test recommendation.
- Optional follow-up after the garage completes a test or repair.

**Commercial model:** Trade hourly/block rate, call-out and travel rules, pre-agreed payment terms and minimum booking value.

## 4.3 Fleet recurring products

The fleet offer must be sold as **condition intelligence and maintenance coordination support**, not as a legal compliance guarantee.

### Fleet Visibility

For micro-fleets. Includes:
- Vehicle register.
- MOT/service/insurance/other date reminders based on customer-supplied or permitted data.
- Defect and issue log.
- Digital FVI report archive.
- Monthly exception summary.
- Annual or scheduled condition visit quoted separately.

### Fleet Insight

For growing small fleets. Includes Fleet Visibility plus:
- Scheduled on-site condition assessments at an agreed frequency.
- Priority booking window.
- Vehicle risk/attention dashboard based on transparent FVI categories.
- Monthly management report.
- Follow-up tracking for reported issues.
- Quarterly review call.

### Fleet Partner

For larger or operationally dependent fleets. Includes a bespoke visit schedule, multi-site planning, fleet trend reporting, agreed response targets, nominated contacts and integration/export options where practical.

**Pricing hypothesis for validation:**
- Platform/admin-only plan: £59–£99 per month for a small fleet, with inspections charged separately.
- Fleet Insight: £149–£299 per month plus any additional inspection volume outside the included allowance.
- Fleet Partner: bespoke from approximately £399 per month, depending on vehicle count, site locations, visit time and reporting scope.

Never sell unlimited call-outs or unlimited inspections at a fixed low monthly fee. Define included hours, vehicles, visit radius, response hours and overage prices in the contract.

## 4.4 Fleet service disclaimer

FVI's initial fleet product is a condition-monitoring and administration support service for cars and light commercial vehicles. It must not be represented as replacing driver daily checks, the operator's roadworthiness responsibilities, statutory inspections, MOT tests or the maintenance system required for a particular vehicle/operator licence. Any formal safety inspection work for regulated commercial vehicles must be separately scoped, performed by competent personnel, at suitable facilities and under an appropriate written arrangement. Check current DVSA requirements before offering that service.

## 4.5 Future specialist services — not launch promises

These can create later differentiation but must only be marketed after competence, equipment, facilities, partner arrangements and insurance are in place:

- ADAS calibration: requires approved procedures, suitable facilities, equipment and training; initially refer to a qualified calibration partner.
- High-voltage EV repair or battery-pack opening: do not offer without the required EV high-voltage competence, PPE, safe systems, tools and insurance. Initial EV offer should be non-invasive health assessment only.
- ECU coding/programming, module replacement and security functions: only where legal access, credentials, equipment and competence are in place.
- DPF cleaning, emissions modification or defeat devices: do not offer unlawful emissions tampering or removal.
- HGV/PSV statutory safety inspections: specialist market with separate competence, facilities, contractual and recordkeeping requirements; not part of the initial consumer/light-fleet offer.

# 5. Pricing and unit economics

## 5.1 Pricing principles

- Charge for diagnostic thinking and evidence, not only time connected to the vehicle.
- Set a minimum call-out/booking value.
- Use geographic zones and clear travel rules.
- Collect a booking deposit for high-value or long-distance appointments.
- Require approval before exceeding the booked diagnostic time block.
- Separate third-party data fees, specialist EV battery test fees and partner services.
- Quote dealer/fleet work based on utilisation and agreed volume, not arbitrary discounts.
- Review realised gross margin monthly.

## 5.2 Example revenue mix (illustrative only)

A mature month might include:

| Revenue stream | Example volume | Illustrative average | Monthly revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Consumer diagnostics and investigations | 18 | £145 | £2,610 |
| Pre-purchase/buyer reports | 8 | £229 | £1,832 |
| Dealer stock visits | 4 site-days | £450 | £1,800 |
| Fleet recurring contracts | 6 | £249 | £1,494 |
| Trade diagnostic cases | 8 | £175 | £1,400 |
| **Illustrative turnover** |  |  | **£9,136** |

This is a planning scenario, not a forecast or promise. It assumes sufficient demand, technician capacity, productive travel routes, equipment coverage and repeat B2B accounts. It is before VAT where applicable, tax, owner remuneration, fuel, insurance, equipment, software, marketing, payment fees, subcontractors and other operating expenses.

## 5.3 Commercial KPI model

Track:
- Revenue by product and channel.
- Gross margin by job type.
- Average job value.
- Paid technician hours versus travel/admin hours.
- Revenue per field day.
- Travel cost per job.
- Quote-to-booking conversion.
- No-show/cancellation rate.
- Repeat customer rate.
- Dealer vehicles inspected per site-day.
- Fleet monthly recurring revenue (MRR).
- Fleet churn and renewal rate.
- Report turnaround time.
- Rework/complaint rate.

Do not optimise solely for number of bookings. Prioritise contribution margin, route density, repeat B2B work and report quality.

## 10.1 Mobile diagnostics page

## 10.2 Advanced diagnostic investigations page

Focus on intermittent electrical problems, network/module communication faults, charging/starting issues and multi-system symptoms where the technician has the competence and equipment. Show exactly what is checked and not checked. ## 10.4 Buyer Due-Diligence Pack page

Any vehicle history, outstanding finance, stolen status, write-off or mileage checks must be supplied through a lawful, licensed data source with the correct terms and customer authority. Make clear what is and is not checked.

## 10.5 EV battery health page

Show the testing provider/method once selected, supported makes/models, how the assessment works, what the report contains, prerequisites, limitations, price and booking process. ## 10.7 Fleet Intelligence page

## 10.8 Dealer Services page

## 10.9 Trade Diagnostic Support page

Target workshop owners and service managers. Explain the cases suited to FVI, required evidence before booking, trade rates/quote process, confidentiality, report handover and boundaries. Add an urgent case request but do not promise 24/7 service unless staffed.

## 10.11 About page

## 10.12 Sample report page

Offer a downloadable sample PDF with fictional or fully anonymised vehicle/customer data. Explain the report structure and limitations.

## 10.13 Location pages

Each town page must have genuinely useful local content, services actually available in that area, travel/availability information and relevant FAQs. Do not create doorway pages or claim the business has a physical branch in towns where it does not.

# 13. Vehicle Intelligence Passport — product requirements

## 13.1 Purpose

Create a customer-authorised record of FVI's inspections and reports for a vehicle over time. It improves customer retention, report portability and dealer/fleet value.

## 13.2 Passport structure

- Chronological FVI reports.

- Condition assessments.

A QR verification page must show only minimum information, for example:
- Report ID.
- Issuing business.
- Issue date.
- Report type.
- Report validity/integrity state.

- Original PDF.
- Report version.
- Created/issued timestamps.

- File checksum/hash.

Set a written retention schedule. Provide a way for customers to request access/correction/deletion where legally appropriate. Retain documents only as long as required for legitimate contractual, legal, insurance and accounting purposes. Explain that deletion may be restricted where records must be retained.

# 18. Technical architecture recommendation

## 18.1 Recommended stack

A modern custom application is recommended if the goal is to develop the Passport and fleet/dealer portals as genuine products rather than stitch together several disconnected plugins.

| Layer | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Frontend | Next.js with TypeScript |
| UI | Tailwind CSS plus accessible component library |
| Backend/API | Next.js server routes/actions or a clearly separated service layer |
| Database | Supabase PostgreSQL |
| Authentication | Supabase Auth with role/organisation membership controls |
| File storage | Private Supabase Storage buckets |
| Hosting | Vercel or equivalent managed hosting |
| Payments | Stripe |
| Email | Resend, Postmark or equivalent transactional provider |
| SMS | Twilio or alternative, only if needed and costed |
| Maps/geocoding | Google Maps Platform or an alternative |
| Analytics | GA4 plus privacy-conscious product analytics |
| Error monitoring | Sentry or equivalent |
| PDF reports | Server-side HTML-to-PDF or a robust PDF generation service |
| Backups | Automated database/storage backups with restore testing |

The stack is a recommendation, not a requirement. Keep business logic and data portable to avoid dependence on one vendor.

## 18.2 Architecture principles

- Mobile-first responsive application.
- Server-side validation for all important operations.
- Database row-level security for private data.
- Role-based and organisation-scoped permissions.
- Private document storage with short-lived signed links.
- Audit trail for issued reports and sensitive actions.
- Rate limiting and bot protection on public forms.
- Backups and tested recovery procedure.
- Secrets stored only in server-side environment variables.
- No API keys, service-role keys or payment secrets in browser code.
- Separate development, staging and production environments.
- Database migrations committed to version control.
- Automated tests for critical booking, payment, access-control and report workflows.

## 18.3 Core integrations

### Stripe

Use hosted checkout/payment elements for deposits and balances. Use signed webhooks to update payment status. Do not mark a booking as paid based only on a browser redirect.

### Email/SMS

Transactional messages for booking and report delivery. Marketing communications require appropriate consent and unsubscribe handling.

### Maps

Postcode validation, service-zone calculation and technician route planning. Do not expose a technician's personal home address.

### Vehicle data providers

If integrating registration lookup, MOT history, recall, finance/write-off, stolen status or valuation data, use a suitable authorised provider/API and comply with its terms. Clearly label source, timestamp and scope. Do not scrape restricted systems or imply access to data not actually retrieved.

### EV battery provider

Integrate or attach the certificate/report from the selected specialist test platform in accordance with its API and commercial terms. Do not reproduce provider marks or claim approval without written permission.

This section is an implementation checklist, not legal advice. Confirm the final service model with an insurer, accountant and appropriately qualified UK legal/compliance adviser where needed.

- Professional indemnity appropriate to inspection/reporting advice.
- Tools/equipment cover.
- Employers' liability if legally required because staff are employed.
- Any EV/high-voltage, diagnostic programming or subcontracting exclusions.

## 22.3 Consumer contracts

Distance/off-premises cancellation rules may apply; obtain legal advice on the exact terms and any request to begin work within a cancellation period.

## 22.4 Inspection limitations

State that an assessment is limited by access, vehicle condition, test conditions, equipment coverage and the agreed scope. No dismantling unless expressly agreed. Any road test depends on seller/owner permission, driver's licence, insurance and roadworthiness/safety. Do not road-test a vehicle where it is unsafe or unlawful.

## 22.5 MOT and statutory inspection distinction

FVI must not imply that a pre-MOT check is an official MOT test. Do not advertise formal statutory HGV/PSV safety inspections unless the business has verified the relevant competence, facilities, contractual arrangements and recordkeeping capability. A fleet support dashboard does not transfer or remove operator/driver responsibility.

## 22.6 EV and high-voltage safety

Start with non-invasive battery health assessment using the appropriate specialist method. Do not open battery packs, disconnect high-voltage systems or carry out high-voltage repairs without the required training, PPE, tools, safe working procedures and insurance. Obtain provider training/approval before using any branded test/certification scheme.

## 22.7 Data protection and security

- Publish a clear privacy notice.
- Document lawful bases for personal data processing.
- Review ICO data protection fee requirements/exemptions.
- Minimise collected data.
- Restrict VIN, registration, location and contact data.
- Use MFA for staff/admin accounts.
- Use encryption in transit and at rest through the selected platform.
- Keep private files in non-public storage.
- Define retention and deletion schedules.
- Maintain a data breach response process.
- Have appropriate processor agreements with hosting, email, payment and software providers.
- Never share a private report publicly by default.

## 22.8 Technical competence

Publish only qualifications, accreditation, training, equipment access and manufacturer/security access genuinely held. Maintain a capability matrix by vehicle/system. Decline or refer work where the required competence or equipment is not available.

# 23. Report quality standard

Every issued report should follow the same core structure.

1. Report ID and version.
2. Client/commissioning party.
3. Vehicle identity and mileage record.
4. Date, time and location.
5. Technician identity.
6. Service ordered and agreed scope.
7. Test conditions and vehicle status.
8. Equipment and methods used.
9. Items checked and items not checked.
10. Findings and severity.
11. Diagnostic fault codes and status, where relevant.
12. Evidence/photos/measurements.
13. Conclusions and confidence level.
14. Recommended next action.
15. Limitations and disclaimers.
16. Issue date and report version.
17. Verification ID/QR code.

## 23.1 Finding terminology

Use controlled language:
- **Observed:** directly seen or measured.
- **Reported:** stated by the customer/seller/driver.
- **Recorded:** retrieved from a diagnostic system or supplied record.
- **Confirmed:** supported by a defined test.
- **Suspected:** plausible but not proven.
- **Not reproduced:** symptom was not recreated during the assessment.
- **Not assessed:** outside scope or access unavailable.
- **Further investigation required:** evidence is incomplete.

This vocabulary is important to protect report quality and prevent overstatement.

# 28. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Market perceives FVI as an expensive scan tool | Demonstrate reports, test process and customer outcomes; sell evidence and investigation, not a code read |
| Too much complexity at launch | Launch with a small, profitable service set; phase portals and integrations |
| Low utilisation due to travel | Set zones, minimum call-out, route-density days and multi-vehicle visits |
| Overpromising diagnosis | Use controlled report language and test limitations; quote staged investigations |
| EV test compatibility gaps | Check provider support before booking/payment; publish coverage accurately |
| Fleet contract underpricing | Define vehicle caps, included hours, site limits and overage fees |
| Liability from inspection wording | Clear scope, competent work, appropriate cover and legal review of terms |
| Report privacy breach | Private storage, role-based access, expiring links and audit logs |
| Dependence on a single dealer/fleet | Diversify account base and set account concentration limits |
| Software build consumes cash before demand exists | Validate manually; build MVP first; automate after repeat demand |
| Technician capacity constraint | Track field hours and report/admin time; add capacity only when margins support it |

# 30. Final product vision

Furness Vehicle Insight should evolve into a regional vehicle intelligence platform with a mobile field operation behind it.

A private customer comes to FVI because a warning light is on or they are about to buy a used car. They receive a useful report and can keep a secure history for the vehicle.

A dealer uses FVI to inspect stock in batches, attach documented findings to each vehicle and provide selected evidence to a buyer.

A local business uses FVI every month to track vehicle condition, open issues, upcoming actions and inspection history.

A garage uses FVI when it needs additional diagnostic expertise and a structured technical handover.

That is the intended differentiation: **FVI does not simply visit vehicles. It creates, explains and maintains useful vehicle evidence.**

The website is the sales and booking front end. The report engine, Passport, fleet dashboard and dealer workspace are the operational products that make the business more defensible and capable of recurring revenue.
